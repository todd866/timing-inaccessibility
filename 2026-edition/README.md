# Timing Inaccessibility and the Projection Bound — 2026 edition

This is a full author’s rewrite of “Timing Inaccessibility and the Projection Bound: Resolving Maxwell’s Demon for Continuous Biological Substrates.” The published article, *BioSystems* 258, 105632 (2025), [doi:10.1016/j.biosystems.2025.105632](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biosystems.2025.105632), remains the version of record and the one to cite. This edition accompanies it as a new presentation of the argument.

The argument now leads with its biological thesis: a continuous substrate coordinates through distributed physical relations without registering every event history, avoiding the demon’s burden of maintaining and resetting those records. Protein folding and neural coordination carry the argument into timing inaccessibility, path degeneracy and the camera–engine account of simultaneous sensing and action.

The structure is sharper and the mathematics clearer. Temporal registration distinguishes fixed-length capacity from order entropy and places the reset conditions beside the heat bound. The Projection Bound follows the physical ensemble, including non-uniform occupancy and entropy within coarse cells. Worked examples make the factorial, timing and configuration scales concrete.

The paper develops deferred projection as an architectural principle: use distributed relations to compute, and retain the records the task requires. Repeated caveats give way to direct explanations, local conditions and a focused experimental programme linking coordination, function and energy over reuse.
